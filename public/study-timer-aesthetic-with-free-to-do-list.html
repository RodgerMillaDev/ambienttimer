The Science Behind the Aesthetic Pomodoro Timers

The science behind a Pomodoro timer lies in the principles of increasing productivity, improving focus, and time management. This time management tool can trace its roots to the late 1980s, thanks to Italian entrepreneur Francesco Cirillo.

The technique requires you to devote a specific amount of time (typically 25 minutes) to a single task with short breaks placed in between. You get to enjoy a longer break (more than 5 minutes) if you complete four cycles.

We’ve been there. We have a big exam coming up in a few days and, for whatever reason, failed to plan our study schedule. As a result, we end up feeling anxious and stressed out because we have too little time and too many lessons to study.

Throughout the years, experts and studies have suggested that the effectiveness of the technique is mostly due to the frequent breaks. This gives your mind an opportunity to feel refreshed, allowing you to resume your task with renewed energy. Also, breaking your study session into smaller chunks makes sure you don’t feel bored, overwhelmed, or easily tired.

When it comes to visuals, it has long been known that what we see has an effect on our mood, thinking, ability to focus, and even energy levels. For instance, bright colors can uplift us, resulting in increased energy and alertness.

Likewise, a Pomodoro aesthetic study timer that has a well-thought-out design—from the background images to the color scheme—can help you get the most out of your study sessions.

Sound speaks to the soul, as they say. So, we have to be careful what we listen to. A study by the Florida National University has shown that listening to ambient music decreases anxiety and stress, which leads to better concentration and brain performance.

Our study timer aesthetic takes advantage of the soothing sounds coming from nature to achieve similar results. You can listen to the birds, breeze, rainfall, and waves to help you experience a state of flow while studying.

Luckily, you don’t need to pour through studies and research to prove the effectiveness of Pomodoro timers. You can use most of them for free, so you can see for yourself if they work or not.

What Can You Do During Pomodoro Breaks?

There are no definite rules on how you should spend your breaks. It’s up to you how you want to spend them. But to give you some ideas, here are some productive things you can do during your short and long Pomodoro breaks.

During Short Breaks

  1. Stretch your body to relieve tense muscles.
  2. Listen to your favorite relaxing or energizing songs.
  3. Make coffee or a hydrating drink.
  4. Perform deep breathing exercises to improve blood flow to your brain.
  5. Organize your desk to prevent loss of focus.
  6. Walk around your room or home.
  7. Play with your pet.
  8. Doodle or draw (if you can).
  9. Eat a healthy and energy-boosting snack.
  10. Go to the bathroom.

During Long Breaks

  1. Go out and take a short walk to clear your mind.
  2. Perform low-impact workouts.
  3. Meditate and/or do yoga.
  4. Take a power nap.
  5. Watch a funny or motivational video.
  6. Think about what you’re going to do in your next study session.
  7. Reflect on your goals.
  8. Engage in a hobby that gives you happiness.
  9. Have a brief and stress-reducing conversation with a friend, family member, or partner.

What Should You Avoid Doing During Pomodoro Breaks?

While we did say you can do whatever you want, please keep in mind that there are some things you need to avoid doing during your breaks. For instance, avoid activities that are mentally and physically draining or could mess with your focus, such as engaging in stressful conversations.

As much as possible, don’t eat heavy meals. This could cause you to feel sluggish or tired when you go back to studying.

Also, reduce your screen time because it can strain your eyes. Plus, you might feel more stressed, instead of more relaxed, by the time your break is over.

Remember: These hard-earned breaks are supposed to recharge your mind and body after long periods of studying.
