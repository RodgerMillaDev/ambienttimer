What Is the Pomodoro Technique?

The Pomodoro Technique is the brainchild of Francesco Cirillo, an Italian developer and entrepreneur. In the late ‘80s, then-university student Cirillo was looking for an effective way to structure his study schedule. He found the answer in a tomato-shaped kitchen timer. That’s why this technique was called pomodoro, which is the Italian word for tomato.

The traditional way of using the technique is quite simple:

  1. Set your Pomodoro study timer for 25 minutes. Use this time to focus on studying a single lesson.
  2. When the timer rings, you can take a short break for 5 minutes.
  3. After that, you repeat the cycle three more times, and then enjoy a longer break for 15 to 30 minutes.

You can organize your study sessions by following the traditional 25/5 approach , like we mentioned above. But most modern Pomodoro timers—whether web-based or app-based—give you the option to set the amount of time you want to dedicate to a single task and rest.

Whether you stick to 25 minutes or not, the primary goal of the Pomodoro study timer stays the same. By dividing tasks into smaller parts, you can focus better and get more done.

What Makes a Pomodoro Study Timer Effective?

In my first and second years of college, I found it difficult to study for long hours. Things got worse when I was studying for finals week. I would often find myself easily distracted, exhausted, and unmotivated.

Something had to change.

Fortunately, I learned about the Pomodoro Technique while surfing the web, particularly this Reddit thread.

After following this technique a couple of times, the results were amazing. I was able to cover more lessons in less time and remember them better. My stress levels also went down, making studying feel less like a chore and more fun.

It turns out this isn't a mere coincidence. Studies show that short bursts of intense study with rest periods are far more effective than long periods of studying.

Paul Kelley, an Oxford University researcher, and his colleagues at the University of Surrey Business School discovered that spaced learning is actually more effective than studying consistently for hours. Spaced learning means taking rest periods while studying to give the brain time to absorb information better.

Their study involved 600 business students, whom they divided into three groups. They gave all groups the same study material, but they learned in different ways. The first group learned through lectures and seminars, while the second group studied on their own. The third group used spaced learning, which consisted of 10-minute breaks.

After a week, the result showed that the spaced learning group remembered 23% more information than the group that studied by themselves and 20% more information than those who learned through the traditional approach (lectures and seminars).

Why is this so?

Kelly and his colleagues believe that spaced learning is effective because it gives the neurons (the brain's messengers) enough time to soak up information. As a result, that information becomes permanently recorded in the brain.

Tips for Maximizing the Benefits of a Pomodoro Study Timer

A 25 minute study timer is effective. But there are other things you can do to maximize its benefits, such as the following:

1. Set a Clear and Achievable Goal

Do this before every start of a Pomodoro session. Think about what you want to achieve during and after studying. This can help you stay focused and motivated. For instance, your goal could be to read two chapters of your textbook within the next 25 minutes.

2. Get Rid of Potential Distractions

Once you know your goal, look around where you plan to study and eliminate possible distractions. These could be your video game console, smart phone, or pet. Make sure to let everyone in your home know not to disturb you while studying.

3. Don't Hesitate to Experiment

Yes, 25 minutes is the standard. But this doesn't mean you're not allowed to adjust it. Don't be afraid to shorten or lengthen your focus sessions and rest periods to find your sweet spot.

4. Stay Dedicated and Flexible

There are times that you might feel like cutting your study short for longer breaks, or vice versa. But you need to fight this urge. Go back to your goals.

As we mentioned earlier, the Pomodoro Technique isn't rigid. If you're in a state of flow, you can continue studying beyond 25 minutes. On the other hand, if you need to take frequent or longer breaks to refresh your mind, adjust the time accordingly.
